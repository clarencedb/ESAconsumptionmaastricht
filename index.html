Liber I

About the Conference

❦

Consumption is a domain in which inequalities become visible, material and lived. What people are able to eat, wear, inhabit, access, repair or share depends on the distribution of economic resources, time, knowledge, infrastructures and social recognition. Consumption can signal and reproduce privilege and exclusion, but it can also become a terrain of solidarity, collective experimentation and demands for greater equality.

The 2027 Midterm Conference of the European Sociological Association's Research Network 05, Sociology of Consumption, invites participants to explore how inequalities are produced, experienced, justified and contested through consumption. It opens with an Early Career Workshop on Monday 18 October, followed by three days of papers, roundtables and working sessions from 19 to 21 October 2027, hosted by the Maastricht Sustainability Institute at Maastricht University's School of Business and Economics.

Under what conditions does consumption reproduce privilege, vulnerability and exclusion, and how might it contribute to more democratic, sustainable and equitable forms of social organisation?

Addressing these questions requires attention not only to differences in what people consume, but also to the conditions that shape and justify them. We propose three entry points.

Caput I

Moral classifications

How consumption is judged

The judgements through which consumption is classed as necessary or excessive, responsible or irresponsible. Who has the authority to define “good” consumption, whose practices gain legitimacy, and how is responsibility shared among individuals, households, businesses and public institutions?

Caput II

Provisioning systems

How access is organised

The institutions, infrastructures, policies, material stocks and labour that make food, housing, energy, mobility and care available, from markets and the state to mutual aid, foraging, gifting and other informal practices that are unevenly accessible and differently valued.

Caput III

Just transitions

How sustainable futures are pursued or denied

Ecological transitions are not automatically fair. A consumption lens asks who benefits, who bears the costs and who shapes change, and what welfare institutions, public infrastructures and collective provisioning can do to make transitions just.

Liber II

Call for Papers

❦

We invite proposals for individual papers, paper sessions, lightning talks, roundtables and working sessions. Contributions may draw on any approach: qualitative, quantitative, computational, historical, participatory, mixed-methods or theoretical. While inequality provides the theme, we warmly welcome contributions from across the full theoretical, methodological and empirical range of the sociology of consumption, and we particularly encourage work from regions and intellectual traditions underrepresented in European consumption research, including Central and Eastern Europe and the Global South.

The call seeks closer dialogue between the sociology of consumption and economic, cultural and environmental sociology, the sociology of labour, social policy and political economy. Possible themes include, but are not limited to:

  • Inequalities, privilege and exclusion in consumption
  • Moralities, valuation and the politics of “good” consumption
  • Infrastructures, welfare and systems of provisioning: food, housing, energy, mobility and care
  • Collective, informal and non-market provisioning: mutual aid, charity, foraging, gifting, commoning
  • Sustainable consumption, its critiques and just transitions
  • Interspecies justice in consumption and provisioning systems
  • Political consumerism, citizenship and social movements
  • Markets, institutions and the political economy of consumption
  • Labour, livelihoods and global supply chains
  • Consumer empowerment and vulnerability
  • Class, capitals and cultural stratification
  • Distinction, identity and taste
  • Gender, race, disability and intersecting inequalities
  • Generations and the life course
  • Changing lifestyles and consumption
  • Geographies of consumer culture
  • Consumption in Central and Eastern Europe and the Global South
  • Digital consumption, platformisation and datafication
  • Materiality and immateriality in consumption
  • Bodies, emotions and the senses in consumption
  • Compulsive and excessive consumption
  • Leisure, tourism and cultural consumption
  • Neglected and emerging topics, theories and methods

Individual paper

15 + 5 minutes

Presentation and Q&A. Papers not submitted with a session are grouped thematically.

Paper session

90 minutes

Up to four papers plus discussion. The organiser submits a session abstract; each presenter submits their own, naming the session.

Lightning talk

up to 5 minutes

A concept, early-stage research or a teaching method, grouped into moderated sessions.

Roundtable

90 minutes

Discussion-based panel in a format chosen by the organisers, e.g. moderated panel or up to five short interventions.

Working session

90 minutes

Develop shared ideas into a plan of action: teaching, internationalisation, large projects, joint publications.

Guidelines for abstracts

  • In English, submitted via Aanmelder
  • A title, a main text of no more than 250 words, and three to five keywords
  • Names, affiliations and email addresses of all authors
  • Each participant may present no more than one paper as presenting author
  • Session, roundtable and working-session proposals: a session abstract of up to 250 words on aims and format, with organisers and confirmed participants

Paper abstracts are evaluated on (1) their sociological background, (2) research questions and methodology, (3) key or expected findings, and (4) implications and contributions.

The conference is hosted by the Maastricht Sustainability Institute (MSI) at the School of Business and Economics (SBE), Maastricht University.

SBE is an international school whose expertise spans economics, international business, management, public policy, governance and sustainability. It holds the “Triple Crown” accreditation (AACSB, AMBA and EQUIS) and was rated a Level 4 “Transforming School” in the 2026 Positive Impact Rating.

MSI is Maastricht University's scientific institute for sustainable development, combining interdisciplinary and transdisciplinary approaches at the interface of science, policy and society. Its work on food systems, provisioning and sustainability transitions connects closely with this conference's theme.
